## Objective

Migrate Customer Master from Blazor to Angular end-to-end, along the full strangler-fig path:
triage and extract the business logic trapped in the pages' `@code` blocks into
`CustomerService`, expose that service as `CustomersController` under `/api/v1/customers`,
and build the Angular list and tabbed editor screens against it — while the Blazor pages stay
live, untouched, and serving the same tenant database throughout.

This parent task is delivered by three sequential children:

1. **M2-D02-01** — `@code` triage + business-logic extraction *(Backend, 4 d)*
2. **M2-D02-02** — `CustomersController` + API tests *(Backend, 3 d)*
3. **M2-D02-03** — Angular list + editor screens + component tests *(Frontend, 4 d)*

Parity between the two UIs is proved separately by **[M2-D03](M2-D03.md)**, which is a
top-level M2 task rather than a child of this one, because it tests the *composition* of
all three children against the still-running Blazor app.

## Scope

The three children named above, in that order, and nothing else. This parent is a container:
it is **never worked directly** ([KB-082](../dependency-graph.md) § *Ready-task selection
rule*). Its content here is the shared context, the sequencing rule and the criteria only the
whole is judged against.

## Why This Task Exists

**M2-D is the vertical slice that de-risks the project, and Customer Master is the half of
it that tells the truth.**

M2-D01 proved the path on Currency, and Currency was chosen because it was easy:
`ICurrencyService` already had `GetByIdAsync`, `CreateAsync` and `UpdateAsync`, so
`CurrencyController` wraps it with **zero service changes** (Confirmed —
`ICurrencyService.cs:12-19`). That is a genuine result, but it is a result about plumbing,
not about the migration's actual problem.

Customer is the actual problem, and it is worth stating the measured numbers:

| File | Lines | `@code` opens | `@code` block | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Customer_Pages/CustomerList.razor` | 984 | `:394` | 591 lines | 60.1 % |
| `Customer_Pages/CustomerUpsert.razor` | 1,309 | `:678` | 632 lines | 48.3 % |
| **Combined** | **2,293** | — | **1,223 lines** | **53.3 %** |

All Confirmed 2026-08-12, and consistent with R-06's system-wide 57 %. Against that:

- **`ICustomerService` has four members and none of them writes a customer** —
  `CanDeleteCustomerAsync`, `SearchWithDynamicFilterAsync`, `DeleteCustomerByCustIdAsync`,
  `GetCustomerListAsync` (`ICustomerService.cs:14-18`, Confirmed). There is no
  `UpsertCustomerAsync` and no `GetCustomerByIdAsync`.
- **The Razor page is acting as the service.** `CustomerUpsert.razor` injects `IUnitOfWork`
  directly (`:13`), opens its own transaction (`:957`), and holds the entire create/update
  path (`:954-1125`) and all validation (`:1127-1214`) in `@code`.
- **`CustomerVM` cannot round-trip its children.** It declares `CustomerIndirectVMs` and
  `ContactPersonVMs` (`:134-135`), but `CustomerMapping` maps neither and the names do not
  match by convention (`CustomerMapping.cs:16-26`). Grepping `V.SMART/` for those two
  property names returns hits **only** in `CustomerVM.cs` — no producer, no consumer.

So Customer cannot be wrapped. It must be **extracted first**, which is exactly what
migration principle 3 mandates and what no task before this one has actually done. That is
why this parent exists as three tasks rather than one: the extraction, the exposure and the
screen are separately reviewable and separately reversible, and the first of them sets the
template for every M3 and M4 wave.

Three further reasons this specific module was chosen as the second slice:

1. **It has child collections.** Two of them. Every ERP document in M3-5 onward is a parent
   with lines; if the controller template and the Angular editor cannot carry a
   parent-with-children through one atomic save, that failure is far cheaper to discover on
   a two-child master than on Sales Order.
2. **It is the template's second independent user.** [KB-080 §9's Definition of Done](../README.md#definition-of-done)
   for M2 requires exactly that: "the template is only real once it has two independent
   users."
3. **It is one of only three entities with an explicit EF configuration** —
   `UserConfiguration`, `ItemConfiguration`, `CustomerConfiguration`
   (`ApplicationDbContext.cs:430`, `:432`, `:434`; `grep -c ApplyConfiguration` returns 3,
   Confirmed). `CustomerConfiguration.cs:16-52` adds eight indexes including a composite
   `(Inactive, CustName)` and a covering index on `CustName`. The team hand-tuned this
   table, which is direct evidence that Customer is high-traffic and worth getting right.

**If the pattern established here is awkward, it is far cheaper to change it now than after
sixty more controllers and forty more screens have copied it.** Saying so is not a caveat
on this task — it is the task's purpose.

**The three children must run strictly sequentially**, and the ordering is not a scheduling
convenience — it is [KB-080 §10's ordering rule](../README.md#10-module-migration-task-pattern)
made concrete: *"step 03 must complete and step 04 must pass before step 08 starts. Building
the SPA screen against un-extracted logic is how ERP behaviour gets silently reimplemented
in TypeScript, which principle 3 forbids."*

Each child is also each of the others' quality gate:

- If **M2-D02-02** finds itself validating, orchestrating or reaching for `IUnitOfWork`
  inside the controller, **M2-D02-01 was incomplete** and the correct response is to send it
  back, not to compensate in the controller.
- If **M2-D02-03** finds itself needing to compute a PAN from a GST, hard-code a `SupTyp`
  list, or issue more than one write request per save, **M2-D02-01 or M2-D02-02 left a
  gap** — and again the fix belongs upstream, not in TypeScript.

## Business Rules

**The `BR-CUST-*` series is allocated by M2-D02-01 and is empty today** (Confirmed —
grepping `docs/kb/` for `BR-CUST` returns no hits, 2026-08-12). No rule is created,
changed or removed by the migration: the whole point of the three-step shape is that the
rules move *location* without moving *meaning*. Two general rules bind every child:

- **BR-AUTH-002** — deny by default. Services carry no authorization; the filter does.
- **BR-CALC-001 / BR-STK-001** — no ERP arithmetic in TypeScript, in any framework.

## Implementation Requirements

1. **Open one child at a time, in order**, each on its own branch. The order is a
   correctness requirement, not a scheduling preference.
2. **Send a child back rather than compensating downstream.** The quality-gate relationships
   above are the mechanism; using them is not a failure, bypassing them is.
3. **Propagate the `BR-CUST-*` ids** allocated by M2-D02-01 into this file's frontmatter and
   into all three children when that child completes.
4. **Keep `CustomerList.razor` byte-identical** throughout. Only `CustomerUpsert.razor`'s
   `@code` is rewired, and only by M2-D02-01.
5. When all three are done, open [M2-D03](M2-D03.md) to prove parity.
